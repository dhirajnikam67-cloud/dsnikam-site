The seat that rarely opens

India's renewable build-out runs through a queue · Grid connectivity · Report No. 4

D. S. Nikam — Independent research, energy storage and power marketsReport No. 4 · Version 1.0 · 17 November 2026 · CTUIL records as at 1 October 2026

India's cheapest path to clean power assumes solar and wind can be added as fast as they are needed. Every one of those megawatts first needs a seat at an inter-State substation. CTUIL publishes lists of connectivity it expects to revoke, and those lists are read as the place where seats come free. We followed every application named on them from June 2025 to March 2026 to where it stands today. About one in ten lost its connectivity. In Gujarat, none did.

1. The cheapest path assumes the seats exist

An open-source national energy-system model published in September 2026 compares four futures for India's power supply to 2041. The cheapest of them has no renewable or storage mandate at all, and no annual cap on solar and wind additions from 2028. Its average cost of supply falls to ₹6.09 a kWh by 2041, the lowest of the four. The pace it needs is high but, the authors argue, within reach: solar additions peak at about 72.5 GW in a single year, against 45 GW added in 2025-26, and wind crosses 50 GW a year in three years.

Among the conditions the same study names for that pace are policies that make it attractive to invest, and transmission connectivity. That second condition is where the build-out meets the grid. In CTUIL's list of 1 October 2026, 43 of the 88 renewable substations with a margin printed have no room left in solar hours, and 7 have no room in any hour. A solar project that cannot find a seat does not get built at any tariff.

2. A revocation list is a warning, not a vacancy

CTUIL publishes, every few months, the connectivity it expects to revoke because a project has not met its dates. The natural reading is that these megawatts are about to come free. Followed to CTUIL's records of October 2026, the 78 applications named on the lists of June 2025 to March 2026 went four ways:

Ninety per cent of what was listed is still held, still waiting, or formally spared. The eight that went belong to four projects — two in Rajasthan, one in Maharashtra, one in Haryana — about 760 MW between them.

3. Deadlines roll forward

A due date on a revocation list is not the date a seat comes free. It is the date by which the next decision is taken, and the decision is usually a new date. Of the 26 applications on the list for August 2025, 17 had already been on the list for June 2025. Of the 23 on the list for October 2025, 8 had been on one of the two before it. Of the 102 entries on today's list, 11 were already on a list in 2025 or early 2026.

One 600 MW solar project shows the pattern. Its date was April 2025 on one list and August 2025 on the next. It appeared on three lists that year. It holds its connectivity today.

4. Four ways off the list

CTUIL's exclusion list up to February 2026 carries eight entries, and each says why it was spared. Three rest on an extension letter, two on an order of the Delhi High Court, and three on a letter from SECI.

A fourth route is paid. Under orders of the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission of 2 March 2026 and 3 August 2026, a grantee facing revocation can keep its connectivity for up to fifteen months from the date that triggered it by paying for the delay. In the compensation statements CTUIL has published under those orders, the daily base rate runs from ₹1,389 to ₹2,773 per MW.

A seat comes free only when none of the four routes is taken. That, not the list, is the event that matters.

5. Gujarat holds; other States release

The split by State is the sharpest finding in the record. Of the 28 Gujarat applications named on the 2025 and early-2026 lists, not one has lost its connectivity: 24 hold it, 3 are still on the watch list and 1 was excluded. Among them are some of the largest grants in the Khavda complex, listed more than once in 2025 and shown as granted in CTUIL's record of October 2026. Of the 50 applications in other States, 8 lost their connectivity — one in six.

A falling number in a margin table is not always a revocation either. In one Gujarat park the connectivity shown as granted fell by 3,000 MW between the July and August 2025 lists, while every project there that had been on a revocation list kept its grant. The table had changed; the seats had not.

6. When a seat does open, it shows — later

At two substations in Rajasthan, the connectivity of a project that left the lists reappears as room for new projects, to the megawatt: 380 MW at one and 70 MW at the other. At both, the margin stood at zero in the lists of mid-2025. The room is in CTUIL's list of August 2026. In the published record, a release and the room it leaves are months apart.

Room that appears may not go to a newcomer. CTUIL's margin list notes that the net margins it shows may include room to be re-allocated to existing applicants in the same complex, and may be used up partly or fully by that exercise. A freed seat can be taken by the queue before anyone outside it sees it.

7. What it means

For a developer looking for a seat. The revocation list is not the opportunity. The opportunity is the small set of entries with no extension, no court order, no agency letter and no compensation behind them — and the time between a seat being released and it appearing in the margin list.

For a lender. A project on a revocation list usually survives. The date it survives to keeps moving, and each move is a fresh condition in the loan file.

For policy. The speed at which India adds renewable energy will depend not only on how many substations are built, but on how quickly a seat that is not being used is released and re-allocated.

The cheapest future in the model is a race for seats. On the record so far, nine in ten seats on the list have not come free.

Limitations

1. Sample. 78 applications over four lists. The Gujarat and other-State figures are what the record shows so far, not a law of nature.

2. Expected, not final. CTUIL's lists are of expected revocations. Where an application is not found in CTUIL's records of October 2026 it is counted as having lost its connectivity.

3. Matches. The link between connectivity that left the lists and new room at the same substation is read from CTUIL's figures and their timing. CTUIL does not state the link.

4. The model. Section 1 reports the published study's own results; they are not re-run here.
