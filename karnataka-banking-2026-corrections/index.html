Karnataka Storage Decision Brief — Corrections

Five corrections to Version 1.3 of 31 August 2026

D. S. Nikam — Independent research, energy storage and power marketsReport No. 1 · Corrections for Version 1.4 · 13 September 2026 · Read Version 1.3

Version 1.3 invited readers to write in if a figure looked wrong, and undertook to record corrections in the next version. Five were found while preparing Report No. 2. All five are my own, and all five are set out in full below, including how each came about.

The headline number changes from ₹5.54 to ₹5.88 per kWh, and the gap over physical storage widens from ₹1.61 to ₹1.95. The conclusion of the brief does not change. It gets stronger.

One citation is withdrawn entirely: the note in Annexure E.1 saying that Civil Appeal No. 9619 of 2019 is a wrong reference. The appeals exist and carry that number.

1. The Supreme Court citation. Withdraw Annexure E.1 in full.

What v1.3 says. Annexure E.1, "And one false trail, closed here", states that Civil Appeal No. 9619 of 2019 is an incorrect citation, that the 9619 is a diary number rather than a Civil Appeal number, and that the judgment attached to it is Anjali and others v. Lokendra Rathod and others, a motor accident compensation matter decided on 6 December 2022.

What is actually the case. The appeals exist and carry that number. They are Civil Appeal Nos. 9619–9637 of 2019, Bangalore Electricity Supply Company Ltd and others v. M/s Fortune Five Hydel Projects Pvt Ltd and others, on the Supreme Court's own record under Diary No. 26531 of 2019, filed 27 July 2019, registered 20 December 2019, CNR SCIN010265312019. They were admitted on 17 December 2019, when notice issued on the prayer for interim relief. On 27 February 2020 the Court directed counter affidavits from two respondents, permitted service on the unserved, and listed the matter for 24 March 2020. No later substantive order was located in the public record as at 13 September 2026, and no order granting interim relief was found.

The Karnataka Commission cites the same numbers itself, in its interim order of 7 September 2021, at paragraph 4(ii) and again in the operative part.

How the error arose, since it is instructive. The diary number in this matter is 26531, not 9619. The number 9619 belongs to the appeal register. A search that treats 9619 as a diary number will land in a different register and return a different case, which is what happened. Anjali v. Lokendra Rathod is a real judgment; it is simply not this one. Two registers, one integer.

Action. Delete Annexure E.1. Delete Limitation 10, which records the case number as unknown. Replace both with the citation above, and add the observation that the appeal has not been listed since February 2020 and that no stay was granted — which is the commercially material fact, and the opposite of what "pending before the Supreme Court" ordinarily suggests.

2. The headline figure of ₹5.54. Not wrong, but stated on a narrower basis than it should be.

What v1.3 says. Banking at 8 per cent, for a consumer with Slot-4 load, is worth ₹5.54 per kWh, against ₹3.93 for physical storage discharged into Slot-3 — a gap of ₹1.61.

What the figure is. It is the season-weighted twelve-month average of the HT-2(a) Slot-4 rate, net of the 8 per cent banking charge:

Slot-4, 22:00–06:00: ₹6.60 in July–November, ₹5.60 in December–June after the 100-paise night rebate

(5 × 6.60 + 7 × 5.60) ÷ 12 = ₹6.0167

₹6.0167 × 0.92 = ₹5.535

That is arithmetically exact and it also resolves the 6.0167 that sat unexplained in the working record. Nothing was mis-keyed.

What is missing from it. The pension and gratuity surcharge of 35 paise, and the fuel and power purchase cost adjustment. Both are recovered per unit and both are displaced by a banked unit, so both belong in the value of one. Including them on the same seasonal blend:

July–November: (6.60 + 0.35 + 0.02) × 0.92 = ₹6.4124

December–June: (5.60 + 0.35 + 0.02) × 0.92 = ₹5.4924

(5 × 6.4124 + 7 × 5.4924) ÷ 12 = ₹5.88

Action. Restate the headline as ₹5.88, with the components shown. The gap over physical storage at ₹3.93 becomes ₹1.95, not ₹1.61, and every derived comparison in section 4 moves with it. The conclusion does not change; it gets stronger.

A caution to carry into the text. Report No. 2 values a Karnataka banked unit at ₹6.41. That is the day slot, 09:00–18:00, in a single month, with the same two components included. It is not a revision of ₹5.88 and the two are not interchangeable. One is a night-load consumer's twelve-month average; the other is a single reference slot. Say so in both publications or they will read as contradicting each other.

3. The Fortune Five judgment can now be cited directly.

What v1.3 says. The judgment was read at pages 1–75, with pages 155–157 taken from a secondary study that quotes them. The brief states that dependency openly, and records at 6.6.3.6 that an earlier version had wrongly declined to attribute natural justice and promissory estoppel to the Tribunal because only the first 75 pages had been read.

What is now available. The full 167-page judgment has been read. The Tribunal's findings are at paragraphs 14.16–14.17, 15.11–15.14, 16.7–16.8 and 17.1–17.3, and the operative order is at page 166. The grounds are: no supervening public interest; breach of natural justice, solar generators never having been noticed although the operative part reached them; promissory estoppel and legitimate expectation, the agreements having guaranteed annual banking for ten years; and no data or analysis of the financial injury on either side. The Tribunal also held the Commission had revisited a question it had already decided and on which it had become functus officio, and applied PTC India Ltd v. CERC — the terms of concluded agreements may be altered by regulations, prospectively, not by an order.

Action. Cite the Tribunal directly throughout and remove the secondary dependency, which also removes the single exception the brief had to carry.

4. A later order deleted part of that judgment. It is not in v1.3 at all.

The judgment of 29 March 2019 remitted the matter to the Commission to pass a fresh order within six months. That direction did not survive. On IA No. 962 of 2019 in Appeal No. 42 of 2018 and batch, the Tribunal by order of 5 August 2019 — Chairperson Justice Manjula Chellur and Technical Member S. D. Dubey — deleted the remanding paragraph from the operative part, holding that since modification of the terms and conditions of a banking arrangement in concluded contracts had been held bad in law and confined to future contracts, nothing remained for fresh consideration.

Why it matters to this brief. It is the reason no fresh Karnataka order on the 2018 reduction was ever passed, and it is the strongest available statement that banking terms in an executed agreement are reachable only prospectively and only by a different instrument.

Action. Add it wherever the 29 March 2019 judgment is discussed, and check every sentence in v1.3 that describes that judgment's effect.

5. What governs the legacy cohort is now on the record.

v1.3 asks, correctly, whether the 8 per cent charge, the month-end lapse and the proposed 31 March 2030 sunset reach the legacy annual-2 per cent cohort, and notes that the draft does not mention that cohort at all. Two documents now answer part of it.

The Commission's interim order of 7 September 2021 continued existing wheeling and banking charges and the annual banking facility, and divided the cohort by commissioning date. Projects commissioned on or before 31 March 2018 with an executed agreement were continued "for the present". Projects commissioned after that date were continued only to 31 March 2022, and only against a written undertaking to abide by the outcome in WA No. 1061 of 2019 and Civil Appeal Nos. 9619–9637 of 2019.

The Commission's order of 7 August 2023 continued annual banking and a charge of 2 per cent of injected energy for Non-REC projects that applied for an agreement between 1 April 2018 and the green open-access cut-off, expressly subject to the Supreme Court's final decision. By letter of 30 March 2023 it had already told the transmission utility and the ESCOMs that an agreement executed with a six-month banking period was to be read as annual.

And a possible answer to the question the brief puts to the Commission. The Forum of Regulators' model State Connectivity and GNA Regulations of January 2026 contain, at Regulation 49.3, a savings clause that preserves existing open-access agreements on their own terms and then carves banking out of that protection by proviso. If Karnataka adopts that structure, the legacy cohort's agreements survive and their banking terms do not. The brief's question can therefore be restated with a candidate answer attached, which is more useful to a reader than the question alone.

Also worth checking before v1.4 issues

The statement that the draft Regulations were published in the Karnataka Gazette on 11 June 2026 is carried in several places with a volume and issue reference. If that reference was taken from the Gazette itself, keep it. If it came from a secondary account, either attach the primary reference or soften it to publication for consultation. Report No. 2 has taken the second course for want of the primary reference.
